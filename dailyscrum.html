What is Daily Scrum, who participates, and what is the objective? What is not Daily Scrum?

The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute time-boxed event held every working day of the Sprint. Its primary objective is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary, adjusting the upcoming planned work. It is a critical daily synchronization point where the team assesses where they are and plans their collaboration for the next 24 hours. For example, a developer might share that they finished coding a login module yesterday and plan to start testing it today.

The primary participants of the Daily Scrum are the Developers. While the Scrum Master and Product Owner can attend as passive listeners, they do not participate unless they are actively executing tasks in the Sprint Backlog. The Scrum Master's role is simply to ensure the event happens and stays within the 15-minute time limit.

It is equally important to understand what the Daily Scrum is not. It is not a status report meeting for management, nor is it a deep-dive problem-solving session. If a complex technical discussion arises—for example, two developers realizing they have a conflict in their code—they should briefly note the impediment and take the detailed discussion "offline" to be solved after the Daily Scrum concludes.
