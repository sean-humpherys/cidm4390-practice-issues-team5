What is sprint execution?

Sprint execution is the phase of a Scrum sprint in which the Development Team carries out the work it committed to during Sprint Planning. A sprint is a fixed, time-boxed iteration, usually one to four weeks, and the team works through the Sprint Backlog to turn selected Product Backlog items into a usable, potentially shippable Increment. The Sprint Goal guides the work, and nothing may be added that would endanger it. During execution the team organizes itself and decides who does what and how. It holds a Daily Scrum, a 15-minute meeting to inspect progress toward the goal and adapt the plan for the next 24 hours. The Scrum Master removes obstacles, and the Product Owner is available to clarify requirements and answer questions about the backlog items.

Throughout the sprint, work is expected to meet the team’s Definition of Done, so completed items are tested, integrated, and of consistent quality. Progress is tracked with tools such as a task board or burndown chart, which make the team’s remaining work visible. Scope may be clarified or renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned, but the sprint length and the quality standards stay fixed. Sprint execution ends when the time-box expires, followed by the Sprint Review, where stakeholders inspect the Increment, and the Sprint Retrospective, where the team looks for ways to improve its process in the next sprint.
